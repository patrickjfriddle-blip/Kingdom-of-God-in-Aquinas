Beatitude

Wis 6:20–21

The Beatitude Sense (Part 7)

English Scripture (Douay-Rheims Challoner)

Therefore the desire of wisdom bringeth to the everlasting kingdom (*ad regnum perpetuum*). If then your delight be in thrones, and sceptres, O ye kings of the people, love wisdom, that you may reign for ever (*ut in perpetuum regnetis*).

Biblia Sacra Vulgata

Concupiscentia itaque sapientiae deducet ad regnum perpetuum. Si ergo delectamini sedibus, et sceptris, o reges populi, diligite sapientiam, ut in perpetuum regnetis.

Thomistic Loci (*The Thomist* 2025 Standard): De reg. I, cap. 9; ST II-II, q. 45, a. 1.
St. Thomas Aquinas: Textus Latinus St. Thomas Aquinas: English Translation & Exegetical Commentary
1. Sensus Literalis (Literal Sense)
«Monitio regibus terrae ut sapientiam divinam quaerant ad regnum obtinendum» (*De reg.* I, cap. 9). Authentic Latin Formula

Admonition to secular rulers that the pursuit of divine wisdom is the only path to an enduring, incorruptible kingdom.

Substantive Exegesis
2. Sensus Spiritualis (Spiritual / Allegorical / Tropological / Anagogical Sense)
«Verum et perpetuum regnum non est temporale sed beatitudo aeterna qua anima fruitur Deo» (*De reg.* I, cap. 9). Authentic Latin Formula

Worldly monarchs must direct their temporal authority toward supernatural beatitude, wherein alone true and eternal royalty is found.
