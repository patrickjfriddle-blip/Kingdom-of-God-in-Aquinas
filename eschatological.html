Part 8

The Eschatological Sense

Consummatio saeculi / Final Judgment & Delivery of the Kingdom
Total Indexed Verses: 9 Loci
Scope: Canonical progression from Old Testament types to New Testament consummation

Thomistic Doctrinal Foundation

Seventhly, the Kingdom of God designates the eschatological consummation of history, the General Judgment, and the final subjugation of all hostile powers (Consummatio saeculi, ST III, q. 59, a. 5; Super I Cor., cap. 15, l. 3). At the end of time, Christ will visibly manifest His judicial kingship, abolish death and every temporal principality, and 'deliver up the kingdom to God and the Father' (1 Cor 15:24), so that God may be all in all (ut sit Deus omnia in omnibus).

Ps 9:8–9 [MT 9:7–8]
Eschatological
 And the Lord remaineth for ever. He hath prepared his throne in judgment (*paravit in iudicio thronum suum*): and he shall judge the world in equity... Super Ps. 9, nn. 7–8; ST III, q. 59, a. 1.
Literal: The Lord's unshakeable throne is established from eternity to execute righteous judgment over the nations.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: The final judicial tribunal of Christ at the Parousia, where the hidden deeds of all men are judged with absolute rectitude.

Ps 9:37 [MT 10:16]
Eschatological
 The Lord shall reign to eternity (*Dominus regnabit in aeternum*), yea, for ever and ever: ye Gentiles shall perish from his land. Super Ps. 9 (LXX 9b/10), n. 16.
Literal: YHWH's perpetual kingship destroys idolatrous pagan nations from the sacred territory of Israel.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: The destruction of carnal vices from the soul, and the final purification of the Church from all hypocrites and unrepentant sinners.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Ps 145:10 [MT 146:10]
Eschatological & Ecclesiological
 The Lord shall reign for ever (*Regnabit Dominus in saecula*): thy God, O Sion, unto generation and generation. ST III, q. 59, a. 2. (Super Ps. 145 deest).
Literal: The Lord God of Zion reigns perpetually over His holy city from generation to generation.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: The eternal reign of Christ in the heavenly Jerusalem, where the communion of saints is confirmed in everlasting peace.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Isa 24:23
Eschatological
 And the moon shall blush, and the sun shall be ashamed, when the Lord of hosts shall reign in mount Sion (*cum regnaverit Dominus exercituum in monte Sion*), and in Jerusalem, and shall be glorified in the sight of his ancients. Super Is., cap. 24; ST Suppl., q. 91, a. 2.
Literal: Cosmic apocalyptic judgment where created luminaries are confounded before the manifest glory of YHWH's reign in Jerusalem.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: The final glory of the New Jerusalem where created sun and moon yield to the uncreated splendor of God, who illumines the blessed forever.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Obad 1:21
Eschatological
 And saviours shall come up into mount Sion to judge the mount of Esau: and the kingdom shall be for the Lord (*erit Domino regnum*). ST III, q. 59, a. 2. (Nullus commentarius directus in Abdiam).
Literal: Prophecy of deliverers ascending Mount Zion to exercise judgment over Edom, establishing YHWH's undisputed rule.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: The final public triumph of Christ and His saints over the worldly and demonic powers at the end of the world.

Matt 25:31–34
Eschatological & Beatitude
 And when the Son of man shall come in his majesty, and all the angels with him, then shall he sit upon the seat of his majesty (*super sedem maiestatis suae*)... Then shall the king say to them that shall be on his right hand: Come, ye blessed of my Father, possess you the kingdom prepared for you (*possidete paratum vobis regnum*) from the foundation of the world. Super Mt., cap. 25, l. 3; ST III, q. 59, a. 5; In Orat., pet. 2.
Literal: The General Judgment: Christ the King publicly sits upon His glorious throne, judging all nations according to works of mercy.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: The ultimate entrance of the elect into the everlasting Kingdom of Glory, eternally prepared for them by the predestination of the Father.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
1 Cor 15:50
Eschatological & Beatitude
 Now this I say, brethren, that flesh and blood cannot possess the kingdom of God (*caro et sanguis regnum Dei possidere non possunt*): neither shall corruption possess incorruption. Super I Cor., cap. 15, l. 7; ST Suppl., q. 82, a. 1.
Literal: Paul asserts that mortal, corruptible human nature cannot inherit the imperishable Kingdom of God without transformation.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: Mortal human nature must be transformed by the power of the resurrection into the spiritualized, impassible body of glory before entering the heavenly Kingdom.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
2 Tim 4:1, 18
Eschatological & Beatitude
 I charge thee, before God and Jesus Christ, who shall judge the living and the dead, by his coming, and his kingdom (*per adventum ipsius, et regnum eius*)... The Lord hath delivered me from every evil work: and will preserve me unto his heavenly kingdom (*salvum faciet in regnum suum caeleste*). Super II Tim., cap. 4, ll. 1, 4.
Literal: Paul charges Timothy in view of Christ's judicial Parousia and kingdom, expressing certitude that the Lord will preserve him for His heavenly kingdom.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: The Second Coming (*adventus*) is the public unveiling of Christ's kingdom, which culminates in the eternal preservation of the elect in beatitude.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Rev 11:15
Eschatological
 The kingdom of this world is become our Lord's and his Christ's (*Factum est regnum huius mundi, Domini nostri et Christi eius*), and he shall reign for ever and ever (*regnabit in saecula saeculorum*). ST III, q. 59, a. 2; In Orat., pet. 2. (Super Apoc. deest).
Literal: The seventh trumpet: the kingdom of this world is definitively subjected to God and His Christ for ever.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: At the close of history, all usurped temporal power, secular kingdoms, and demonic dominion are totally subjected to Christ's public and eternal reign.
