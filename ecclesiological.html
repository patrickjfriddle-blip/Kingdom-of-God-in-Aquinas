Part 4

The Ecclesiological Sense

Ecclesia militans / The Church on Earth, Hierarchy & Keys
Total Indexed Verses: 20 Loci
Scope: Canonical progression from Old Testament types to New Testament consummation

Thomistic Doctrinal Foundation

Thirdly, the Kingdom of Heaven signifies the Church Militant on earth (tertio dicitur congregatio iustorum, scilicet Ecclesia militans, Super Mt., cap. 3, l. 1). In this present age, the kingdom is a mixed body (corpus permixtum) containing both good and bad, wheat and tares, until the final separation at the harvest (Matt 13:24–30, ST III, q. 8, a. 3). Christ has endowed this visible society with the keys of authority, pastoral jurisdiction, and sacramental powers entrusted to the Apostles and their successors under Peter (Matt 16:18–19, De regno I, cap. 14).

Ecclesiological & Christological

Dan 7:18, 27
Ecclesiological & Beatitude
 But the saints of the most high God shall take the kingdom (*suscipient regnum sancti Dei altissimi*): and they shall possess the kingdom for ever and ever... And that the kingdom, and power, and the greatness of the kingdom (*regnum, et potestas, et magnitudo regni*), under all the heaven, may be given to the people of the saints of the most High: whose kingdom is an everlasting kingdom (*cuius regnum sempiternum est regnum*), and all kings shall serve him, and shall obey him. ST III, q. 59, a. 5; Super I Cor., cap. 6, l. 1.
Literal: The apocalyptic promise that the holy people of God will ultimately receive the universal dominion formerly held by oppressive beasts.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: The elect receive the kingdom by union with Christ, sharing initially in His royal authority on earth and consummately on thrones of glory in heaven.

Matt 5:19–20
Ecclesiological & Beatitude
 He therefore that shall break one of these least commandments... shall be called the least in the kingdom of heaven (*in regno caelorum*)... unless your justice abound more than that of the scribes and Pharisees, you shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven (*non intrabitis in regnum caelorum*). Super Mt., cap. 5, l. 2; ST I-II, q. 107, a. 1.
Literal: The perfection of the New Law requires internal observance of the heart surpassing the externalism of the Pharisees.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: 'Kingdom of heaven' denotes either the Church Militant (where an unfaithful teacher is esteemed least) or the Church Triumphant (entered only by those possessing infused justice).

Matt 8:11–12
Ecclesiological & Beatitude
 And I say to you that many shall come from the east and the west, and shall sit down with Abraham, and Isaac, and Jacob in the kingdom of heaven (*in regno caelorum*): But the children of the kingdom (*filii autem regni*) shall be cast out into the exterior darkness... Super Mt., cap. 8, l. 2; ST II-II, q. 10, a. 1.
Literal: Gentiles will enter the heavenly banquet of the patriarchs through faith, while unbelieving children of the covenant are excluded.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: The historical Jews who possessed the type and promise of the kingdom are cast out for rejecting Christ, while Gentiles enter the reality of the Church and glory.

Matt 13:24–30, 36–43
Ecclesiological & Eschatological
 The kingdom of heaven is likened (*Simile factum est regnum caelorum*) to a man that sowed good seed in his field... The Son of man shall send his angels, and they shall gather out of his kingdom (*de regno eius*) all scandals, and them that work iniquity... Then shall the just shine as the sun, in the kingdom of their Father (*in regno Patris eorum*). Super Mt., cap. 13, ll. 2, 4; ST II-II, q. 10, a. 8; ST III, q. 8, a. 3.
Literal: Parable of the Weeds: good seed and weeds co-exist in the field until the harvest at the close of the age.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: Refutes Donatism: 'His kingdom' in this age is the Church Militant as a mixed body (*corpus permixtum*); at the Last Judgment, sinners are separated, and the just enter the Kingdom of Glory.

Matt 13:31–33
Ecclesiological & Mystical
 The kingdom of heaven is like (*Simile est regnum caelorum*) to a grain of mustard seed... The kingdom of heaven is like (*Simile est regnum caelorum*) to leaven... Super Mt., cap. 13, l. 3.
Literal: Parables illustrating the disproportionate growth of the Kingdom from minute origins to universal expansion.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: The mustard seed is Christ and the apostolic preaching, small in humble appearance but growing into the universal Church; the leaven is divine charity transforming the whole soul.

Matt 16:18–19
Ecclesiological
 And I say to thee: That thou art Peter; and upon this rock I will build my church... And I will give to thee the keys of the kingdom of heaven (*claves regni caelorum*). And whatsoever thou shalt bind upon earth, it shall be bound also in heaven... Super Mt., cap. 16, l. 2; ST Suppl., qq. 17–20; Contra Graec. II, cap. 32.
Literal: Christ promises Peter ministerial stewardship, conferring the keys of authority to govern the Church Militant.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: The keys signify spiritual power of order and jurisdiction, whereby apostolic ministers open heaven to the faithful through sacramental absolution and the removal of the debt of sin.

 Therefore receiving an immoveable kingdom (*regnum immobile suscipientes*), we have grace, whereby we may serve God pleasing him, with fear and reverence. Super Heb., cap. 12, l. 5.

 Amen, amen I say to thee, unless a man be born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God (*non potest videre regnum Dei*)... unless a man be born again of water and the Holy Ghost, he cannot enter into the kingdom of God (*non potest introire in regnum Dei*). Rev 20:4–6
Ecclesiological
 And they lived and reigned with Christ a thousand years (*regnaverunt cum Christo mille annis*)... they shall be priests of God and of Christ; and shall reign with him a thousand years (*regnabunt cum eo mille annis*). ST Suppl., q. 77, a. 1; ST III, q. 59, a. 5. (Super Apoc. deest).
