Part 5

The Mystical Sense

Regnum gratiae / Iustitia fidei in the Soul
Total Indexed Verses: 14 Loci
Scope: Canonical progression from Old Testament types to New Testament consummation

Thomistic Doctrinal Foundation

Fourthly, St. Thomas identifies the Kingdom of God as the interior reign of grace and the justice of faith within the soul (quarto dicitur iustitia fidei, secundum illud: Regnum Dei intra vos est, Super Mt., cap. 3, l. 1; Collationes in Orationem Dominicam, a. 2). In his master exegesis of Romans 14:17 (ST I-II, q. 108, a. 1, ad 1), Thomas demonstrates that the Kingdom does not consist in external observances such as food and drink, but in infused justice (iustitia), interior peace of conscience (pax), and supernatural joy in the Holy Ghost (gaudium), whereby God reigns uncontested over the human appetites.

Num 23:21
Mystical Type
 The Lord his God is with him, and the sound of the victory of the king (*clangor victoriae regis*) in him. ST I-II, q. 102, a. 4; Super Ps. 46, n. 2.
Literal: Balaam confesses that YHWH is present as the supreme royal Leader and Victor among the camps of Israel.

Spiritual: Signifies the indwelling of God in the soul of the just, whose grace grants continuous triumph over the assaults of demonic powers.

Job 36:7
Mystical & Beatitude
 He will not take away his eyes from the just, and he placeth kings on the throne for ever (*reges in solio collocat in perpetuum*), and they are exalted. Super Iob, cap. 36, l. 1.
Literal: Elihu explains that divine providence never loses sight of the righteous, elevating them to royal honor.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: God grants the just interior spiritual dominion over their passions in this life, and seats them upon everlasting thrones of heavenly glory.

Ps 96:1–2 [MT 97:1–2]
Mystical & Eschatological
 The Lord hath reigned (*Dominus regnavit*), let the earth rejoice: let many islands be glad. Clouds and darkness are round about him: justice and judgment are the establishment of his throne (*iustitia, et iudicium correctio sedis eius*). ST I-II, q. 100, a. 5; In Orat., pet. 2. (Super Ps. 96 deest).
Literal: The earth and distant islands rejoice at the manifestation of God's sovereign reign and transcendent holiness.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: The interior joy of the believer when the Kingdom of Grace is established within the soul, rooted upon the foundation of divine justice.

Wis 10:10
Mystical
 She conducted the just, when he fled from his brother's wrath, through the right ways, and shewed him the kingdom of God (*ostendit illi regnum Dei*), and gave him the knowledge of the holy things... ST II-II, q. 180, a. 3, ad 4; Super Io., cap. 1, l. 16.
Literal: Wisdom guides Jacob fleeing from Esau and grants him the visionary ladder at Bethel (*regnum Dei*).
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: Jacob's ladder typifies the contemplative ascent of the soul to spiritual union with God and intuitive knowledge of celestial mysteries.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Matt 6:10, 33
Mystical, Beatitude & Eschatological
 Thy kingdom come (*Adveniat regnum tuum*). Thy will be done on earth as it is in heaven... Seek ye therefore first the kingdom of God (*primum regnum Dei*), and his justice, and all these things shall be added unto you. In Orat., pet. 2; Super Mt., cap. 6, ll. 2, 6; ST II-II, q. 83, a. 9.
Literal: The Lord's Prayer: petitioning the establishment of God's sovereign will and prioritizing the Kingdom above temporal goods.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: Thomas distinguishes: (1) Manifestation of Christ's universal dominion over all creatures (eschatological), (2) The glory of Paradise (anagogical), (3) God reigning in the soul through grace rather than sin (tropological).
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Matt 13:44–50
Mystical, Beatitude & Ecclesiological
 The kingdom of heaven is like (*Simile est regnum caelorum*) unto a treasure hidden in a field... is like to a merchant seeking good pearls... is like to a net cast into the sea, and gathering together of all kind of fishes... Super Mt., cap. 13, l. 4; ST II-II, q. 186, a. 3.
Literal: Parables of the Hidden Treasure, Pearl of Great Price, and Dragnet.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: The treasure and pearl represent the contemplative enjoyment of divine truth and beatitude, for which a man renounces all earthly goods; the dragnet is the Church gathering all men until final judgment.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Matt 18:1–4
Mystical & Beatitude
 Who, thinkest thou, is the greater in the kingdom of heaven (*in regno caelorum*)? ...Whosoever therefore shall humble himself as this little child, he is the greater in the kingdom of heaven (*in regno caelorum*). Super Mt., cap. 18, l. 1; ST II-II, q. 161, a. 1.
Literal: Disciples dispute over precedence in the expected messianic realm; Christ corrects them by setting a child in their midst.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: True greatness in the Kingdom of Grace is measured not by temporal dominion, but by innocent humility and charity devoid of pride and ambition.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Matt 19:23–24
Mystical & Beatitude
 Amen, I say to you, that a rich man shall hardly enter into the kingdom of heaven (*intrabit in regnum caelorum*). And again I say to you: It is easier for a camel to pass through the eye of a needle, than for a rich man to enter into the kingdom of heaven (*intrare in regnum caelorum*). Super Mt., cap. 19, l. 2; ST II-II, q. 186, a. 3.
Literal: Christ warns that attachment to earthly wealth makes entrance into the Kingdom of Heaven morally impossible for human power.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: Riches entangle the affections in the earthly city; only the grace of God can detach the heart to enter the narrow portal of celestial beatitude.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Mark 4:26–29
Mystical
 So is the kingdom of God (*Sic est regnum Dei*), as if a man should cast seed into the earth, and should sleep, and rise, night and day, and the seed should spring, and grow up whilst he knoweth not. Cat. in Mc., cap. 4, l. 4.
Literal: Parable of the Seed Growing Secretly: the earth produces fruit of itself through hidden, gradual growth.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: The interior, mysterious operation of divine grace in the soul, where the infused virtues mature from initial conversion to perfect charity without human comprehension.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Mark 10:14–15
Mystical & Beatitude
 Suffer the little children to come unto me, and forbid them not; for of such is the kingdom of God (*talium est enim regnum Dei*). Amen I say to you, whosoever shall not receive the kingdom of God (*non receperit regnum Dei*) as a little child, shall not enter into it. Cat. in Mc., cap. 10, l. 2; ST II-II, q. 161, a. 1.
Literal: Christ welcomes little children, presenting them as the pattern for entering the Kingdom of God.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: Receiving the Kingdom as a child signifies spiritual innocence and the utter absence of malice, deceit, and worldly ambition.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Luke 17:20–21
Mystical & Christological
 The kingdom of God cometh not with observation (*Non venit regnum Dei cum observatione*): Neither shall they say: Behold here, or behold there. For lo, the kingdom of God is within you (*ecce enim regnum Dei intra vos est*). Cat. in Lc., cap. 17, l. 5; ST I-II, q. 108, a. 1; De reg. I, cap. 14; In Orat., pet. 2.
Literal: Christ refutes carnal Pharisaic expectations of an earthly, visible political kingdom: *intra vos est*.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: (1) Christ Himself (*Christus ipse*) is standing in their midst, or (2) the Kingdom is interior, consisting in the righteousness, peace, and grace of the Holy Spirit in the soul.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Rom 14:17
Mystical
 For the kingdom of God is not meat and drink (*Non est enim regnum Dei esca et potus*); but justice, and peace, and joy in the Holy Ghost (*iustitia, et pax, et gaudium in Spiritu Sancto*). Super Rom., cap. 14, l. 2; ST I-II, q. 108, a. 1, ad 1; In Orat., pet. 2.
Literal: Paul establishes that the New Law of the Gospel does not place the Kingdom of God in ceremonial dietary regulations.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: Thomas's master text for the internal Kingdom of Grace: it consists in infused justice (*iustitia fidei*), peace of conscience, and spiritual joy produced by the Holy Ghost.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Col 1:13
Mystical & Christological
 Who hath delivered us from the power of darkness, and hath translated us into the kingdom of the Son of his love (*transtulit in regnum Filii dilectionis suae*). Super Col., cap. 1, l. 3; ST III, q. 49, a. 1; In Orat., pet. 2.
Literal: God has rescued believers from the authority of darkness and transferred them into the kingdom of His beloved Son.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: Justification is literally a spiritual translation from the tyrannical empire of Satan to the Kingdom of Christ, governed by filial love and sanctifying grace.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
James 2:5
Mystical & Beatitude
 Hath not God chosen the poor in this world, rich in faith, and heirs of the kingdom which God hath promised to them that love him (*haeredes regni, quod repromisit Deus diligentibus se*)? ST II-II, q. 162, a. 1; ST III, q. 40, a. 3. (Super Iac. deest).
Literal: James condemns partiality toward the rich, declaring that God chose the poor in the world to be rich in faith and heirs of the promised kingdom.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: Voluntary poverty and detachment from temporal wealth dispose the soul to be enriched with supernatural faith and charity, securing title to celestial beatitude.
