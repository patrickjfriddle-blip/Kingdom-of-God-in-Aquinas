Part 7

The Beatitude Sense

Ecclesia triumphans / Curia caelestis & Eternal Life
Total Indexed Verses: 14 Loci
Scope: Canonical progression from Old Testament types to New Testament consummation

Thomistic Doctrinal Foundation

Sixthly, the Kingdom of Heaven is the celestial court and eternal beatitude of the saints in heaven (quinto dicitur curia caelestis et beatitudo aeterna, Super Mt., cap. 3, l. 1; In Orat. Dom., pet. 2). This is the kingdom promised to the poor in spirit (Matt 5:3) and the righteous who persevere unto the end (Matt 25:34). It consists essentially in the Beatific Vision—the direct, intuitive contemplation of the divine essence (ST I, q. 12, a. 1; ST Suppl., q. 93, a. 1)—wherein the elect reign with Christ in everlasting rest and glory.

Wis 3:8
Beatitude
 They shall judge nations, and rule over people, and their Lord shall reign for ever (*regnabit Dominus illorum in perpetuum*). ST Suppl., q. 89, a. 1; ST III, q. 59, a. 5.
Literal: The vindication and reward of the righteous after mortal trials, reigning perpetually with God.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: The glorified saints participate in Christ's judicial and royal authority, sitting as assessors with Him at the Last Judgment.

Wis 6:20–21
Beatitude
 Therefore the desire of wisdom bringeth to the everlasting kingdom (*ad regnum perpetuum*). If then your delight be in thrones, and sceptres, O ye kings of the people, love wisdom, that you may reign for ever (*ut in perpetuum regnetis*). De reg. I, cap. 9; ST II-II, q. 45, a. 1.
Literal: Admonition to secular rulers that the pursuit of divine wisdom is the only path to an enduring, incorruptible kingdom.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: Worldly monarchs must direct their temporal authority toward supernatural beatitude, wherein alone true and eternal royalty is found.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Matt 5:3, 10
Beatitude & Mystical
 Blessed are the poor in spirit: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven (*regnum caelorum*)... Blessed are they that suffer persecution for justice' sake: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven (*regnum caelorum*). Super Mt., cap. 5, l. 1; ST I-II, q. 69, a. 2.
Literal: The Beatitudes: Christ promises the Kingdom of Heaven to the humble and to those who endure persecution for justice.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: Promised in the present tense (*est*) because humility already possesses the initial seed of the Kingdom through grace, which blossoms into celestial beatitude.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Matt 7:21
Beatitude
 Not every one that saith to me, Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of heaven (*intrabit in regnum caelorum*): but he that doth the will of my Father who is in heaven, he shall enter into the kingdom of heaven (*intrabit in regnum caelorum*). Super Mt., cap. 7, l. 3; ST II-II, q. 4, a. 5.
Literal: Verbal confession of Christ without moral obedience to the Father does not admit a man into the Kingdom of Heaven.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: Formless faith (*fides informis*) is insufficient for salvation; entrance into beatitude requires faith animated by charity (*fides formata*) and meritorious works.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Matt 20:20–23
Beatitude & Christological
 Say that these my two sons may sit, the one on thy right hand, and the other on thy left, in thy kingdom (*in regno tuo*)... but to sit on my right or left hand, is not mine to give to you, but to them for whom it is prepared by my Father. Super Mt., cap. 20, l. 3; ST III, q. 59, a. 5.
Literal: The mother of James and John seeks places of royal honor for her sons in Christ's kingdom.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: Christ refutes carnal nepotism: heavenly thrones are distributed according to the Father's eternal predestination and conformity to Christ's Passion, not human favoritism.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Matt 26:29
Beatitude
 And I say to you, I will not drink from henceforth of this fruit of the vine, until that day when I shall drink it with you new in the kingdom of my Father (*in regno Patris mei*). Super Mt., cap. 26, l. 4; ST III, q. 73, a. 5; ST III, q. 83, a. 4.
Literal: Christ concludes the Last Supper, abstaining from wine until the Kingdom of the Father is realized.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: The Eucharistic banquet of the Church Militant is a pledge and type of the celestial feast in heaven, where the elect enjoy the 'new wine' of direct beatific fruition.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
1 Cor 6:9–10
Beatitude
 Know you not that the unjust shall not possess the kingdom of God (*iniqui regnum Dei non possidebunt*)? Do not err: neither fornicators, nor idolaters, nor adulterers... shall possess the kingdom of God (*regnum Dei non possidebunt*). Super I Cor., cap. 6, l. 2; ST I-II, q. 88, a. 1; ST II-II, q. 118, a. 4.
Literal: Paul catalogues grave sins that legally exclude a person from inheriting the Kingdom of God.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: Mortal sin immediately destroys sanctifying grace, which is the necessary title of divine adoptive sonship required to inherit the Kingdom of Glory.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Gal 5:21
Beatitude
 Envy, murders, drunkenness, revellings, and such like. Of the which I foretell you, as I have foretold to you, that they who do such things shall not obtain the kingdom of God (*regnum Dei non consequentur*). Super Gal., cap. 5, l. 6; ST I-II, q. 70, a. 4.
Literal: Warning that practicing the works of the flesh deprives a man of obtaining the Kingdom of God.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: The works of the flesh extinguish charity, whereas only the Fruits of the Spirit preserve the soul in grace to inherit eternal beatitude.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Phil 3:20–21
Beatitude
 But our conversation is in heaven (*Nostra autem conversatio in caelis est*); from whence also we look for the Saviour, our Lord Jesus Christ, who will reform the body of our lowness, made like to the body of his glory, according to the operation whereby also he is able to subdue all things unto himself. Super Philip., cap. 3, l. 3; ST I-II, q. 65, a. 5; De reg. I, cap. 14.
Literal: The Christian's citizenship (*conversatio/politeuma*) resides in heaven, awaiting Christ who will transform our bodies.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: While living in mortal flesh within earthly kingdoms, the believer belongs spiritually to the heavenly city (*civitas Dei*), seeking eternal beatitude as his ultimate end.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
1 Thess 2:12
Beatitude
 That you would walk worthy of God, who hath called you unto his kingdom and glory (*vocavit vos in suum regnum et gloriam*). Super I Thes., cap. 2, l. 2.
Literal: Exhortation to the Thessalonians to live a moral life worthy of God, who calls them to His heavenly kingdom and glory.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: God summons the elect not to an earthly, perishable commonwealth, but to participate eternally in His own celestial kingship and beatific glory.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
2 Thess 1:5
Beatitude
 For an example of the just judgment of God, that you may be counted worthy of the kingdom of God (*digni habeamini in regno Dei*), for which also you suffer. Super II Thes., cap. 1, l. 1; ST I-II, q. 114, a. 3.
Literal: Present persecutions suffered by the faithful are proof of God's just judgment, preparing them to be worthy of His kingdom.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: Patient endurance under trials merits the Kingdom of God *de condigno* through the grace of Christ, purifying the soul for celestial glory.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Heb 11:10, 16
Beatitude
 For he looked for a city that hath foundations: whose builder and maker is God... But now they desire a better, that is to say, a heavenly country (*meliorem appetunt, id est, caelestem*). Super Heb., cap. 11, l. 3; ST I-II, q. 104, a. 1; De reg. I, cap. 14.
Literal: Abraham and the patriarchs lived as pilgrims on earth, seeking the eternal city founded and built by God.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: Thomas emphasizes that human final beatitude cannot be found in any temporal earthly kingdom, but exclusively in the heavenly *patria* of the City of God.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
2 Pet 1:11
Beatitude & Christological
 For so an entrance shall be ministered to you abundantly into the everlasting kingdom of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ (*in aeternum regnum Domini nostri, et Salvatoris Iesu Christi*). ST III, q. 59, a. 2. (Super II Pet. deest).
Literal: Peter encourages growth in virtues to secure abundant entrance into the eternal kingdom of Jesus Christ.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: Explicitly designates the Kingdom of Glory as the eternal kingdom of Christ, into which entrance is merited through perseverance in the theological and moral virtues.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Rev 22:3–5
Beatitude
 And the throne of God and of the Lamb shall be in it (*thronus Dei et Agni in ea erunt*), and his servants shall serve him. And they shall see his face: and his name shall be on their foreheads... and they shall reign for ever and ever (*regnabunt in saecula saeculorum*). ST I, q. 12, a. 1; ST Suppl., q. 93, a. 1; In Orat., pet. 2. (Super Apoc. deest).
Literal: The final vision of the New Jerusalem: the throne of God and of the Lamb, where His servants see His face and reign for ever.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: The ultimate consummation of the biblical revelation: the Beatific Vision, wherein the glorified elect participate immutably in God's eternal kingship.
