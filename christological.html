The Christological Sense

Christus ipse / Autobasileia & Davidic Types
Total Indexed Verses: 45 Loci
Scope: Canonical progression from Old Testament types to New Testament consummation

Thomistic Doctrinal Foundation

In his commentary on St. Matthew (Super Mt., cap. 3, l. 1), St. Thomas states that the Kingdom of Heaven is said first and preeminently of Christ Himself (primo dicitur Christus ipse), who is the Kingdom in person (Autobasileia, as Origen also noted). In the Old Testament, this kingdom is prefigured in the royal Davidic covenant (2 Sam 7, Ps 2, Ps 71, Ps 88, Ps 109) and the prophecies of the Messiah-King (Isa 9, Dan 7, Mic 5, Zech 9). In the New Testament, Christ reveals His sovereign power through His miracles, His passion (reigning from the wood of the Cross, John 12), and His resurrection and ascension, possessing universal judicial and royal authority over all creatures (Matt 28:18, ST III, q. 59, aa. 1–4).

 Jesus therefore, when he knew that they would come to take him by force, and make him king (*ut raperent eum, et facerent eum regem*), fled again into the mountain himself alone. Super Io., cap. 6, l. 2; ST III, q. 59, a. 4; De reg. I, cap. 14.
Literal: Following the multiplication of loaves, the crowd attempts to force Jesus into temporal kingship; Jesus flees alone to the mountain.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: Major thesis locus: Christ explicitly rejects earthly, temporal kingship; His kingdom is not secular (*non temporale sed spirituale*), and its public manifestation is deferred until after the redemption and judgment.

John 12:12–15
Christological
 Blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord, the king of Israel (*rex Israel*)... Fear not, daughter of Sion: behold, thy king cometh (*ecce rex tuus venit*), sitting on an ass's colt. Super Io., cap. 12, l. 3.
Literal: Royal acclamation of Jesus entering Jerusalem on Palm Sunday, fulfilling Zechariah 9:9.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: Christ permits royal acclaim not for temporal pride, but to signify that His throne is the Cross, where His spiritual kingship over the Gentiles is inaugurated.

John 18:36–37
Christological & Evangelical
 Jesus answered: My kingdom is not of this world (*Regnum meum non est de hoc mundo*). If my kingdom were of this world, my servants would certainly fight... but now my kingdom is not from hence (*regnum meum non est hinc*). Pilate therefore said to him: Art thou a king then (*Ergo rex es tu*)? Jesus answered: Thou sayest that I am a king (*Tu dicis quia rex sum ego*). For this was I born, and for this came I into the world; that I should give testimony to the truth. Super Io., cap. 18, l. 6; ST III, q. 59, a. 4; De reg. I, cap. 14; Sent. II, d. 44, q. 1, a. 3.
Literal: Christ's formal confession before Pontius Pilate: His kingdom is not derived from this world and does not compete with Caesar's empire, but rules over minds through divine truth.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: Primary thesis locus: Christ's kingdom is *in* this world as to its subjects, but not *of* this world as to its origin; it does not usurp secular authority or depose temporal rulers, but establishes spiritual authority over souls.

Rev 1:5–6, 9
Christological & Ecclesiological
 The prince of the kings of the earth (*Princeps regum terrae*), who hath loved us, and washed us from our sins in his own blood, and hath made us a kingdom, and priests to God and his Father (*fecit nos regnum, et sacerdotes*)... I John, your brother and your partner in tribulation, and in the kingdom, and patience in Christ Jesus (*particeps in tribulatione, et regno*)... ST III, q. 59, a. 1; ST III, q. 22, a. 1, ad 3. (Super Apoc. deest).
Literal: Doxology praising Christ as *Princeps regum terrae*, who has redeemed believers by His blood and made them a kingdom and priests to God.
📖 Aquinas Latin & English Text ➔
Spiritual: Christ's supreme kingship transcends all earthly sovereigns; by the communication of His Passion, the Church participates in His royal and priestly character.

Christological & Mystical
 Now is come salvation, and strength, and the kingdom of our God, and the power of his Christ (*regnum Dei nostri, et potestas Christi eius*): because the accuser of our brethren is cast forth... ST III, q. 49, a. 1. (Super Apoc. deest).

Christological
 These shall fight with the Lamb, and the Lamb shall overcome them, because he is Lord of lords, and King of kings (*Rex regum*)...
